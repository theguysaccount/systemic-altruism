## Original 6 October 2026 seed audit (historical)

Reviewed 6 October 2026. This is a proposed open research method and a 40-organization seed cohort, not an exhaustive ranking of all charities or an independent audit of their effectiveness. The seed is intended to make the hypothesis, evidence, uncertainty and next test visible.

## Existing work and the actual contribution

[Giving Green's 2025–2026 list](https://www.givinggreen.earth/top-climate-nonprofits) explicitly selects organizations for systemic-change potential. Its list is alphabetical, not a rank order. Its [systems-change research](https://www.givinggreen.earth/research/how-and-why-we-think-about-systems-change-as-a-climate-funder) describes policy, innovation, market shaping and grassroots work that change incentives beyond an individual project. [Founders Pledge's Climate Fund](https://www.founderspledge.com/programs/climate-fund/about) similarly funds neglected opportunities to alter trajectories and systems. [Animal Charity Evaluators](https://animalcharityevaluators.org/charity-reviews/evaluating-charities/) evaluates impact, room for more funding and organizational health, including corporate and policy mechanisms. We should credit this work and avoid claiming that no one evaluates systemic change.

The useful contribution is a cross-cause, inspectable tracker: a public source trail; explicit intervention scope; separable design and evidence judgments; user-adjustable values; missing data; and a record of what would confirm or falsify a systemic-change claim. It should complement specialist evaluators, not relabel their recommendations as our independent findings.

## Cohort and scope

The JSON includes 40 organizations: 8 global health, 8 poverty and opportunity, 7 climate and environment, 10 animal welfare, and 7 justice and public goods. Inclusion is purposive, not random or comprehensive. ## Proposed rubric v0.1

The six axes below describe **systemic design potential**, not demonstrated welfare impact. A high design score can accompany weak evidence or harmful implementation. These are contestable normative choices, and should be displayed as editorial hypotheses. Weights are proposed defaults, not empirically estimated multipliers.

| Axis | Default weight | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Depth | 30% | No articulated path to durable change | Direct symptom relief or prevention | Changes a recurring local practice or resource constraint | Alters organizational rules, public-service routines or sector practices | Explicitly targets interconnected public rules, incentives, rights or shared infrastructure |
| Reach | 20% | No identified population or institution | Individual or small local group | Locality or selected national communities | National/regional institutions or several countries | International rules, markets or public infrastructure with broad potential application |
| Durability | 20% | Benefit ceases immediately with delivery | Benefit requires frequent replacement or recurring operations | Skills, routines or voluntary commitments could persist but need active upkeep | Institutional adoption, equipment, legal rules or capacity could persist beyond the grant | Credible long-term institutional persistence and enforcement demonstrated across stress or turnover |
| Scalability | 15% | No plausible replication route | Highly individual and constrained delivery | Replicable but labor/capital/context intensive | Repeatable model or shared institutional lever | Public digital knowledge/tool with low marginal reuse, or an independently demonstrated scaling pathway |
| Additionality | 10% | Audited evidence shows displacement/no useful marginal funding | Likely crowded or already financed, with specific evidence | Documented unmet need but uncertain marginal contribution | Current gap and delivery capacity supported by credible counterfactual reasoning | Independently validated bottleneck with convincing evidence marginal resources unlock additional outcomes |
| Agency | 5% | Audited coercion or exclusion from consequential decisions | Consulted with limited consequential authority | Meaningful feedback and shared design rights documented | Affected people control important resources or representatives have material decision rights | Affected people have enforceable governance/ownership and robust accountability rights |

A missing score is **null/unknown**, never zero. Unknown does not mean an organization lacks that quality. All additionality values and most agency values are null because this seed did not audit current funding gaps, marginal capacity or beneficiary governance. GiveDirectly's recipient control of cash and Wikimedia's community role are narrowly described; they do not establish control of the whole organization's budget.

The seed's scores are preliminary interpretation of documented mechanisms. Reach is the potential scope of an institutional lever, not a count of people already helped. Durability is conditional potential unless independently demonstrated. No entry receives durability 4 because long-term stress-tested persistence was not established by this review. Large numerical reach, publicity, overhead ratios, incorporation status and publication counts must not stand in for causal impact.

## Aggregation, missingness and rankings

For complete records the proposed design index is `100 × sum(weight × axis/4) / sum(weight)`. Evidence remains alongside this index and is not hidden inside it. For incomplete records, the preferred display is a bounded interval plus assessed-weight coverage: compute a lower bound with missing axes at 0 and upper bound with missing axes at 4 **solely to express uncertainty**, while continuing to show those raw axes as unknown. These bounds are mathematical possibilities, not empirical confidence intervals. Do not label a lower bound a definitive score. Another acceptable display is an observed-axis subtotal with its denominator and explicit missing axes. Do not silently renormalize missing axes into an apparently complete 100-point score.

Only compare entries within this reviewed cohort and under the same chosen weights. Present ties and uncertainty rather than artificial precise positions. A cross-cause score reflects donor values; a health-prevention charity may have stronger causal evidence despite a lower systemic-design score. Systemic work and immediate relief can be complementary. Do not imply that a low structural-depth score means a low-value charity.

## Evidence and confidence stay separate

Use two distinct questions: “Is this mechanism documented?” and “Did this organization cause the claimed benefit?” The seed's `evidenceLevel` labels reviewed source type, not a formal causal evidence grade. Its `confidence` is low or limited for organization-specific systemic attribution, even for evaluator-selected entries.

A future causal evidence scale can use 0: no verifiable claim; 1: self-reported activities; 2: independently verified outputs/adoption; 3: credible quasi-experimental or triangulated contribution evidence with counterfactuals; 4: independently replicated causal evidence plus sustained outcomes. An evaluator review may contain evidence at several levels and must not automatically map to level 4. A randomized trial of nets, cash or vaccination does not validate every long-term systems claim or all of an organization's programs.

## Falsifiable review workflow

Each profile includes a directional claim to test. Before elevating it to a finding, record an outcome, baseline, population, timeframe, counterfactual, independent measurement and precommitted success/failure threshold. Follow the chain separately: activity → adoption → implementation → outcomes → durability. A pledge is not compliance, a law is not enforcement, an audience is not behavior change, a database is not better decisions, and a market's growth is not substitution.

For policy: record exact adopted provisions, contribution evidence, implementation/enforcement and affected-group outcomes. For animal welfare: verify supplier compliance and welfare rather than pledge counts. For climate: measure additional lifecycle emissions effects and avoid counting both charity and government attribution at 100%. For livelihoods: measure persistence after assistance and effects on nonparticipants. For public goods: evaluate use, quality, accessibility and independently measured decision or rights effects. Record downside pathways, displacement, political reversals, rights harms and ecological risks. Invite correction and charity/community response without granting the evaluated organization editorial veto.
